CMT-Transformer: A Probabilistic Task-Coupled Framework with Empirical Prior Integration for Cognitive Rehabilitation Decision Support

The original data, experiments, and writing for this paper were all completed by Yunhui Zhang.

Abstract Cognitive rehabilitation decision support involves transforming heterogeneous assessment information into cognitive-status estimates and downstream rehabilitation-domain predictions. Existing multi-task approaches often treat related tasks as parallel objectives or rely on hard-label transfer, limiting their ability to represent asymmetric dependencies and probabilistic information flow. This study proposes CMT-Transformer, a probabilistic task-coupled framework for joint cognitive-status stratification and rehabilitation-domain prediction. The framework integrates self-supervised representation learning, Transformer-based feature modeling, probabilistic task conditioning, and empirical prior integration. Unlike hard cascades, CMT-Transformer transfers the complete cognitive-status probability distribution as task-level information, preserving relative support for alternative assessment states during downstream prediction. A training-derived assessment-rehabilitation prior is incorporated into the domain logits through a learnable fusion coefficient while retaining sample-specific representations. Experiments on a cohort of 5,099 older adults derived from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study showed that CMT-Transformer achieved a mean accuracy of 0.8899 for cognitive-status stratification and a mean average precision of 0.9535 for rehabilitation-domain prediction, yielding the strongest mean performance across the reported metrics. Ablation studies, sensitivity analyses, statistical comparisons, and computational evaluations further characterized the contributions of probabilistic task coupling and empirical prior integration. An interactive web-based system was developed to demonstrate the complete inference workflow. The framework provides a practical approach for modeling directional dependence between cognitive assessment and rehabilitation-domain prediction in structured decision-support settings.
